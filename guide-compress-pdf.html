How to Compress a PDF on Android

"The file is too large to attach." Almost everyone has hit that wall. Compressing a PDF shrinks its size so it slips under email and messaging limits, uploads faster, and takes up less space — while keeping the document readable.

Why PDFs get so big

Most oversized PDFs are heavy because of images: high-resolution photos, scanned pages, or screenshots embedded at full quality. A three-page scanned contract can easily be 15–20 MB. Compression re-encodes those images at a more sensible resolution and quality level, which is where the size savings come from.

Step-by-step: compressing a PDF

  1. Open the Compress tool in PDF Manager from the home screen.
  2. Select your PDF. Pick the large file you want to shrink from your device.
  3. Let it process. The app re-encodes the document on-device — no upload to any server.
  4. Check the before/after comparison. You'll see the original size next to the new size, so you know exactly how much you saved.
  5. Save the smaller file. Keep it as a new file so your original stays intact in case you need full quality later.
Tip: Compression can reduce file size by up to about 80% on image-heavy PDFs. Text-only PDFs are already small, so they won't shrink much — that's normal, not a bug.

When to compress (and when not to)

  • Do compress before emailing scans, uploading to a portal with a size cap, or sending over chat apps.
  • Be cautious with documents intended for large-format printing, where maximum image detail matters — keep an uncompressed master copy.
  • Skip it for PDFs that are already small (a few hundred KB); there's little to gain.

Frequently asked questions

Will compression make my text blurry?
Text stays crisp — it's vector-based. Only embedded images are re-encoded, so readability is preserved for normal on-screen and standard printing use.
Does it work offline?
Yes. Compression happens on your device, so your file is never uploaded.
Can I compress a file I just merged or scanned?
Absolutely. A common workflow is scan → merge → compress, then send.
